The parallel version is SLOWER than the serial one. That is a
real result, not a bug: this scan is short enough that doubling
the work and paying the dispatch cost outweighs splitting it.
The parallel scan wins on larger inputs or more cores; below the
crossover the correct choice is the serial scan.

The serial scan is WORK-efficient: N additions, the theoretical
minimum. But it is not STEP-efficient at all, because every one of
those N additions waits for the previous one. Its critical path is N
steps long, so no number of cores can help it.

The chunked scan shortens the critical path to roughly N/p plus the
12-element reduction, at the price of doing 2N additions
instead of N. That is the trade in the task's title. The classic
textbook pair makes the same point more sharply: Hillis-Steele scan
finishes in O(log n) steps but performs O(n log n) work, while
Blelloch scan keeps work at O(n) and takes 2 log n steps. One buys
latency with work, the other buys work with latency.

Note how little communication the chunked version needs: one integer
per chunk, 12 numbers total out of 2,000,000. The dependency that
makes prefix sum look inherently sequential turns out to bind only
inside a chunk; across chunks it compresses to a single offset.
