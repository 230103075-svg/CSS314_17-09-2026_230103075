==============================================================================
ANALYSIS
==============================================================================
Completion delta is the honest measure here. It is the time cores
spend idle at the barrier waiting for the slowest worker, and it is
exactly what a scheduler is supposed to drive to zero.

  Static  delta : 9.325 ms
  Dynamic delta : 1.367 ms (best of chunk 1 and 5)
  Reduction     : 85.3%

Predicted static delta from the cost model : 10.000 ms
Measured static delta                      : 9.325 ms
The two agree closely, which confirms the measured imbalance comes
from how the work was divided and not from OS scheduling noise.

Static scheduling fixes the assignment before the first item runs, so
it can only be as good as its guess about cost. Here the cost repeats
every 20 items and each block of 250 covers 12 and a half full cycles,
so the blocks come out nearly, but not exactly, equal. Whatever
imbalance remains is paid in full: a worker that finishes early cannot
help, it just waits.

Dynamic scheduling makes no guess. A worker that finishes early comes
back for more, so the imbalance can only ever be as large as the last
chunk still in flight. That is why chunk = 1 gives the tightest finish
spread: the worst case is one item, 1.9 ms. Chunk = 5 claims five items
per lock acquisition, trading a slightly looser spread for fewer trips
through the shared queue.

The cost of dynamic scheduling is that shared queue. Every chunk claim
takes a lock, so chunk = 1 pays 1,000 lock acquisitions against chunk
= 5's 200. When the items are cheap relative to that overhead, dynamic
can lose on total time even while winning on the delta, which is the
trade-off behind OpenMP's schedule(static) / schedule(dynamic, n) /
schedule(guided) choice.
